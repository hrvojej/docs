# O001 — KONČAR Transformer Intelligence & Lifecycle Service

## Thesis

Productize existing KONČAR capabilities into a unified offering for **continuous transformer health, fleet intelligence and lifecycle service**, rather than selling TMS primarily as a monitoring subsystem.

Potential product stack:

**KONČAR TMS / sensors → secure edge → fleet data layer → health/risk/prognostics → expert remote monitoring → field intervention → lifecycle SLA**

## Why this thesis exists

B001 shows a mature global category with:
- Hitachi TXpert;
- Reinhausen ETOS/TESSA;
- Siemens Energy / Camlin;
- ABB TRAFCOM;
- specialist monitoring vendors.

KONČAR already possesses much of the underlying technology:
- Institute TMS + iPDCore;
- D&ST transformer design/manufacturing and international installed base;
- Digital SCADA/OT/cyber/data platforms;
- NEOS data engineering/ML;
- HELB field installation/testing/service.

Therefore the core question is productization and business model, not basic feasibility.

## Target buyers

Initial buyer groups to validate:
1. TSOs / DSOs;
2. renewable operators with critical HV/MV transformers;
3. industrial plants;
4. data centres / critical facilities;
5. power generators;
6. D&ST customers;
7. owners of aging mixed-vendor transformer fleets.

## Jobs-to-be-done

Customers buy outcomes such as:
- avoid catastrophic/unplanned failure;
- reduce routine site visits and manual sampling;
- prioritize maintenance;
- safely extend asset life;
- defer replacement CAPEX;
- optimize loading and cooling;
- prioritize fleet investment;
- document condition/risk;
- receive expert action recommendations rather than raw alarms.

## Proposed product ladder

### Tier 1 — Digital Ready / Retrofit
- TMS edge package;
- selected sensors;
- remote connectivity;
- standard protocols;
- dashboard/reporting.

Commercial model:
- hardware + engineering + commissioning.

### Tier 2 — Fleet Health
- central multi-site fleet view;
- standardized health indices;
- anomaly/risk ranking;
- remaining-life/prognostics;
- asset documentation/history;
- automated reports.

Commercial model:
- software license/subscription + annual support.

### Tier 3 — Expert Monitoring
- periodic expert review;
- event/alarm triage;
- defined escalation process;
- maintenance recommendations;
- remote diagnostics.

Commercial model:
- annual per-asset/per-fleet service fee.

### Tier 4 — Lifecycle SLA
- defined response times;
- planned site interventions;
- periodic diagnostic campaigns;
- maintenance planning;
- HELB/Institute field services.

Commercial model:
- 3–5 year service agreement.

### Tier 5 — Outcome / Performance Contract
Later-stage option only after actuarial/failure-risk economics are understood:
- availability/risk-based guarantees;
- monitoring bundled with lifecycle maintenance;
- potentially “transformer intelligence as a service”.

Do not launch this tier before risk/pricing capability is mature.

## Evidence of KONČAR scale already available

Institute reporting:
- 780 online monitoring systems delivered;
- 64 countries;
- >60,000 MVA monitored by TMS.

This means the initial commercialization problem is not “obtain first reference”.

It is “convert references and installed systems into a scalable lifecycle platform and recurring service”.

## Competitive differentiation hypotheses

Potential KONČAR differentiation:
1. **OEM + vendor-neutral** — own transformer manufacturing while supporting mixed fleets.
2. **physics/domain + AI** — Institute diagnostics plus NEOS/Data capabilities.
3. **OT-native** — PROZA/SCADA/critical-infrastructure cyber capability.
4. **field execution** — HELB/service layer.
5. **regional proximity** — CEE/SEE utility relationships.
6. **equipment breadth** — future extension from transformers to switchgear, generators, motors and rail assets.

Do not claim these as proven customer differentiators until buyer interviews.

## Strategic boundary

For v1, prioritize **D&ST + Institute + Digital/NEOS + HELB**.

Treat KPT separately because:
- Siemens Energy owns 51%;
- Siemens Energy competes directly in transformer monitoring/digitalization;
- IP/data/commercial rights may be constrained.

## Candidate MVP

A realistic MVP should mostly integrate existing products rather than create new sensing technology.

Proposed pilot structure:
- 10–30 transformers across 1–3 willing customers or controlled/pilot environments;
- mix of existing TMS and newly retrofitted units;
- central fleet dashboard;
- standardized health/risk index;
- event workflow;
- monthly expert report;
- defined field escalation path.

Exact assets/customers must be validated before selection.

## Commercial model hypotheses to test

Revenue stack:
1. hardware / TMS / sensor package;
2. engineering and commissioning;
3. recurring fleet-software subscription;
4. recurring expert-monitoring fee;
5. field diagnostics/maintenance;
6. multi-year SLA;
7. later performance/outcome contracts.

Expected strategic benefit:
- more recurring/service revenue;
- increased lifetime value of D&ST customers;
- stronger installed-base relationship;
- potential retrofit revenue on non-KONČAR transformers.

No margin or revenue number is validated yet.

## Competition / urgency

Competitive intensity is high.

Hitachi, Reinhausen, Siemens/Camlin and ABB already sell integrated monitoring/service offerings. The window is not based on invention of a new category; it is based on whether KONČAR can convert its existing technology and regional/domain position into a sufficiently differentiated offering.

## Validation gates before E5

### Customer
- 10–15 interviews across utilities/renewables/industry;
- identify current spend and pain;
- willingness-to-pay for expert monitoring/SLA;
- purchasing owner: maintenance, asset management, operations or capex.

### Internal
- TMS product roadmap and P&L;
- current fleet-software architecture;
- current remote service capability;
- D&ST sales attach rate;
- HELB international service capacity;
- data rights/cyber requirements.

### Economics
- TMS hardware ASP/margin;
- installation cost;
- cloud/hosting cost;
- annual expert labour per asset;
- expected ARR per transformer/fleet;
- SLA incident cost;
- customer avoided-outage/maintenance value.

### Pilot
- identify 1 internal/friendly owner fleet;
- establish telemetry/data rights;
- define 6–12 month success metrics.
